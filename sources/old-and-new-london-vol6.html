Walford — Old and New London, Vol. VI: The Southern Suburbs

Edward Walford (1823–1897) — Old and New London: A Narrative of Its History, Its People, and Its Places. Vol. VI. The Southern Suburbs. New edition, carefully revised and corrected. London: Cassell & Company, Limited.

Concluding volume of the six. Walford states at the outset that the work’s last circuit will keep mainly to metropolitan and strictly suburban districts south of the Thames (with a final west-bank coda up through Fulham, Hammersmith, and Chiswick). Southwark and Bankside supply deep history—bridge, abbey church, theatres, bears—before the chapters fan out through dockland, Greenwich, the southern residential belt, Lambeth and the “transpontine” theatres, and the south-western villages absorbed into Greater London.

Content of the volume

Southwark

Introductory Southwark: geology of the south bank, etymology, Roman settlement, old London Bridge, Knut’s trench, Norman reception, civic annexation to the City, saga material on Olaf and the bridge. Continued chapters: old London Bridge in detail; St. Saviour’s (Southwark Cathedral); Winchester House and Barclay’s brewery; Bankside in the old time—Hope and Swan theatres, Paris Garden, bear-baiting, Pepys and Evelyn, bad repute and royal visits; High Street; St. Thomas’s and Guy’s Hospitals. This block is the volume’s historical deep end.

Bermondsey and Rotherhithe

Tooley Street and Bermondsey trades; the Abbey; Rotherhithe (Redriff), manor descent, fires, docks and sailor parish—downstream working river.

Greenwich and Blackheath

A full Greenwich sequence: town; Hospital for Seamen; parish church; Park and Royal Observatory. Then Blackheath, Charlton; Eltham, Lee, Lewisham—court, naval charity, science, and suburb on the Dover road side.

Inner southern residential belt

Old Kent Road; Newington and Walworth; Camberwell; Peckham and Dulwich; Sydenham, Norwood, and Streatham (including Crystal Palace/Paxton associations after the Hyde Park Exhibition); Brixton and Clapham; Stockwell and Kennington. These chapters chart the filling-in of south London as commuter and middle-class residence.

St. George’s Fields, Blackfriars Road, Lambeth

St. George’s Fields and Bethlehem Hospital; Blackfriars Road with Surrey Theatre and Surrey Chapel; Lambeth—parochial history, manor, appearance from the Adelphi, Lambeth Palace associations, then transpontine theatres and Waterloo Road. Political meetings and open-ground memory sit beside ecclesiastical Lambeth.

Vauxhall to Chiswick; conclusion

Vauxhall and Battersea (gardens, fields, bridges, Red House); Wandsworth; Fulham (Walham Green, North End); Hammersmith; Chiswick (eyot, church, Hogarth and gardening names). A general remarks and conclusion chapter closes the entire Old and New London enterprise—looking back across six volumes of street narrative.

Use for this wiki

Gives the south-bank and suburban metropolis missing from Westminster-centred political sources: bridge and Borough, naval Greenwich, Crystal Palace south, Lambeth as archiepiscopal and theatrical shore, and the continuous town that reformers, railway companies, and the LCC inherited. Read with Volumes III–V for a full Victorian mental map of the capital.
