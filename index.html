United Kingdom 1865–1919 Wiki

This knowledge base follows the political, imperial, and social history of the United Kingdom from the death of Palmerston to the aftermath of the Great War. It is built from contemporary and near-contemporary sources—McCarthy’s History of Our Own Times, the Annual Register, Clayton on the rise of democracy, Bagehot on the money market, Thornbury and Walford’s Old and New London on the capital’s streets and institutions, Langer on the diplomacy of imperialism, Mackinder on British geography and sea power, Churchill and Corbett on the naval war—so that narrative pages stay within what those texts actually say.

The story arc runs through four great movements. First, the mid-Victorian settlement: Reform Acts that widened the franchise, Gladstone and Disraeli alternating at the head of party government, the Crown adapting to a democratic age, and London’s money market underwriting commercial primacy. Second, the new imperialism of the 1880s–1900s: the scramble for Africa, Egypt and the Sudan, the South African War, and the consolidation of an empire whose defence rested on command of the sea. Third, the Edwardian crisis of party and constitution: tariff reform and free trade, the Liberal landslide of 1906, old-age pensions and National Insurance, the People’s Budget and the Parliament Act, the great labour unrest of 1911–14, Labour representation, women’s suffrage, and the Irish Home Rule struggle that brought the kingdom to the edge of civil war. Fourth, the Great War: the Anglo-German naval race that preceded it, the outbreak of 1914, Dogger Bank and the first Coalition of 1915, Gallipoli, the Easter Rising and conscription in 1916, the Somme, Jutland, Passchendaele, the Representation of the People Act of 1918, the German spring offensive and the Allied counter-offensive, and the Armistice that closed the fighting without settling the world that followed.
